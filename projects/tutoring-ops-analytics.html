01 · Problem

How can tutoring records support review without replacing professional judgment?

The project organizes synthetic session evidence around progress status, session consistency, follow-up needs, and topic planning. Its purpose is to demonstrate a defensible analytics workflow, not an automated tutoring-management system.

02 · Data and provenance

One row represents one synthetic tutoring session.

Features include subject, topic, grade band, session length, homework completion, attendance status, prior and current scores, engagement, parent follow-up, days since session, exam pressure, topic difficulty, confidence self-report, and cancellation history.

A generator-only concept-mastery variable is retained for audit and excluded from the model feature set. Missing engagement and confidence remain missing during feature construction so preprocessing can fit imputation values on the training partition rather than the full dataset.

03 · Related targets

Progress review and no-show risk are modeled separately.

The main target classifies progress as improving, stable, or needs_attention. A secondary model estimates synthetic no-show-risk categories. Keeping the targets separate prevents operational risk from being presented as the same question as learning progress.

04 · Evaluation design

Simple models remain competitive and visible.

The project retains majority, logistic-regression, random-forest, engineered-feature, and transparent-rule comparisons. The final experiment table selects base logistic regression for the main target; the engineered variants do not improve the result. The robustness script derives its model choice from the current evidence rather than hard-coding a preferred algorithm.

Robustness checks compare random-row, grouped-student, and temporal date-index proxy splits. A real deployment would also need tutor grouping, genuine timestamps, and prospective validation.

The learned model improves on the rule baseline, but not by enough to justify an inflated automation story. The practical evidence is the auditable review workflow, the training/test boundary discipline, and the willingness to choose the simpler model.

06 · Decision policy

Outputs route to review actions, not automatic intervention.

The policy layer can produce an on-track marker, progress review, session-risk review, tutor follow-up, next-topic review, context request, or coordinator review. The model does not replace tutor judgment.

07 · Limitations

The project models same-row synthetic labels, not proven future outcomes.

  • The labels are generated from simplified assumptions about mastery, attendance, engagement, and scheduling.
  • Progress and no-show-risk are contemporaneous synthetic labels, not validated forecasts of future outcomes.
  • No real student progress, attendance, learning gain, fairness, or business impact is demonstrated.
  • Temporal date-index evaluation is a proxy, not a prospective deployment test.
  • Any real version would need governed records, privacy review, reliable timestamps, tutor/coordinator labels, grouped student and tutor splits, monitoring, and human override.
