Portfolio boundary

These projects demonstrate process—not real-world validity.

All three datasets and targets are synthetic. The projects do not prove learner benefit, mental-health meaning, attendance prediction, tutoring impact, causal effects, business lift, or production readiness.

Working method

An inspectable chain from assumptions to action

The portfolio is designed so a recruiter can follow the story and a technical interviewer can follow the evidence.

  1. 01

    Frame

    Define the question, unit of observation, target, user, and forbidden claim.

  2. 02

    Generate and validate

    Make synthetic assumptions explicit, check schemas, and preserve audit-only latent fields outside the model feature set.

  3. 03

    Compare

    Retain majority, linear, tree, and transparent-rule baselines so complexity has a reference point.

  4. 04

    Stress-test

    Use grouped and temporal proxy splits, class and slice diagnostics, calibration or abstention, and concrete error cases.

  5. 05

    Constrain the decision

    Separate model output from a cautious policy layer and record what remains unverified.

Research foundation

Mathematical training shapes how I review analytical claims.

My doctoral research in set theory required precise definitions, explicit assumptions, proof construction, and defensible technical writing. The same habits carry into data work: a model score matters only when its target, sampling process, split strategy, and limits are clear.

With John Krueger, I coauthored “Some Results on Non-Club Isomorphic Aronszajn Trees,” published in the Notre Dame Journal of Formal Logic in 2022.
